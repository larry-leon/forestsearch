# Identifying a Stronger-Benefit Subgroup to Achieve Chinese-Subpopulation Consistency in MRCTs: A Literature Review

## TL;DR
- **The exact question you are posing — deliberately identifying a stronger-benefit subgroup so that the Chinese (or Asian) subpopulation of an MRCT meets consistency criteria — is real, has at least one concrete regulatory precedent (luspatercept/BELIEVE, restricted China label ≤15 transfusion units/24 weeks), and is now being formalized in two 2026 preprints (Qing, Ren, Xu & Yu, arXiv:2602.07468, "two-step" covariate-shift consistency; and the four-question ICH E17 heterogeneity workflow, arXiv:2605.16885) — but the statistical machinery for *post hoc, data-driven* subgroup rescue with type-I-error/selection-bias control specific to consistency claims remains largely an open problem.**
- **The dominant, regulator-accepted paradigm is covariate-*explained* (not covariate-*searched*) consistency: pre-specified effect modifiers under ICH E17/E5, MHLW Method 1 (Japanese effect preserves ≥50% of the global effect, with ≥80% assurance) and Method 2 (same direction in all regions), and their descendants (Ko/Quan/Chen/Tanaka/Teng; DREM; empirical shrinkage; random-effects consistency probability with regional sample-size calculation in the 2025–2026 Statistics in Medicine / Pharmaceutical Statistics papers).**
- **For a methodologist, the highest-value gap is a bias-corrected, pre-specifiable exploratory subgroup-search tool that outputs a consistency ratio (δ_region/δ_global) or CATE-standardized regional estimate with honest post-selection inference; the complementary, safer route when the subgroup story is fragile is information borrowing (Bayesian dynamic borrowing with robust MAP/power priors; Bayesian model averaging), which already has China bridging precedent (GSK mepolizumab).**

## Key Findings

**(a) Papers most directly addressing "identify a stronger-benefit subgroup to make the Chinese subpopulation consistent."** Three works sit squarely on the intersection:
- **Qing, Ren, Xu & Yu (2026), "Consistency Assessment of Regional Treatment Effect for Multi-Regional Clinical Trials in the Presence of Covariate Shift," arXiv:2602.07468 [stat.AP].** The single most on-point paper. It reframes consistency around the *conditional* average treatment effect (CATE) rather than the marginal region-specific ATE and proposes a **two-step assessment** using a density-ratio adjustment to remove covariate shift between the region and the rest of the trial. It uses BELIEVE/luspatercept as its worked example: the global odds ratio was 5.79 (95% CI 2.24–14.97), the Asian subgroup (n=117) was non-significant at OR 3.06 (95% CI 0.64–14.63), but "a subgroup of the Asian population consisting of 68 patients who had a baseline RBC transfusion burden (BTB) between 6 and 15 units per 24 weeks… the responder rate (21.7% vs. 4.5%) and estimated OR (5.83) closely mirrored the global results… This led to the conditional approval of luspatercept in China… restricted to 'baseline transfusion burden ≤15 units/24 weeks.'" This is the formalization of "subgroup/covariate rescue" of regional consistency. (MSD/Merck-funded.)
- **"A Workflow for Evaluating Regional Treatment Effect Heterogeneity in Multi-Regional Clinical Trials," arXiv:2605.16885 (2026).** A structured four-question framework aligned to ICH E17: (Q1) is there heterogeneity; (Q2/Q3) which covariates explain the region-by-treatment interaction; (Q4) what remains unexplained. It uses DR-learner cross-fitted CATE estimation with region as a covariate and connects to the PLATO interaction-decomposition approach (Carroll & Fleming 2013; Dane et al. 2019).
- **Hua, Hong & Wang (2024), "Inference of treatment effect and its regional modifiers using restricted mean survival time in multi-regional clinical trials," arXiv:2404.08128 [stat.ME].** Uses inverse-probability-of-sampling and calibration weighting to strip out *exogenous* regional covariate imbalance while preserving *intrinsic* differences (race, genetics), with RMST as the effect measure — directly relevant to a survival methodologist working on regional modifiers.

**(b) Regulatory precedents and outcomes.**
- **Luspatercept (Reblozyl), BELIEVE (Cappellini et al., NEJM 2020;382(13):1219–1231), transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia — POSITIVE precedent.** Global responder 21.4% vs 4.5%, OR 5.79 (95% CI 2.24–14.97). Asian subgroup (n=117) inconsistent/non-significant: 14.8% vs 5.6%, OR 3.06 (95% CI 0.64–14.63). Post-hoc subgroup of 68 Asian patients with baseline transfusion burden 6–15 units/24 weeks restored OR 5.83 (21.7% vs 4.5%), mirroring global. BTB treated as effect modifier → **conditional China approval restricted to "baseline transfusion burden ≤15 units/24 weeks."**
- **Ticagrelor (Brilinta), PLATO (Wallentin et al., NEJM 2009; Mahaffey et al., Circulation 2011;124:544–554) — CAUTIONARY precedent.** Global HR 0.84 (0.77–0.92); North American region ran in the wrong direction (prespecified region-by-treatment interaction p=0.045). Per Mahaffey et al.: "Of 37 baseline and postrandomization factors explored, only aspirin dose explained a substantial fraction of the regional interaction," with a stark imbalance — "More patients in the United States (53.6%) than in the rest of the world (1.7%) took a median aspirin dose ≥300 mg/d" — while the authors cautioned that a clopidogrel-favoring result in ≥1 of 4 prespecified regions "could occur with 32% probability, [so] chance alone cannot be ruled out." The post-hoc nature delayed FDA approval and produced a black-box warning on aspirin dose ≥100 mg; Taiwan TFDA approved (2012) conditional on a mandatory post-approval Taiwanese study.
- **TOPCAT (Pitt et al./Pfeffer et al.; regional analyses in Circulation 2015) — CAUTIONARY.** ~4-fold event-rate difference between Russia/Georgia and the Americas; spironolactone showed benefit in the Americas (primary HR 0.82, 0.69–0.98) but not Russia/Georgia (HR 1.10, 0.79–1.51; interaction p=0.12). The canrenone-metabolite analysis (de Denus, O'Meara, Desai et al., NEJM 2017;376(17):1690–1692) found "Canrenone levels were undetectable in 30% of participants from Russia, as compared with only 3% from the United States and Canada" (P<0.001), concluding "the trial results obtained in Russia do not reflect the true therapeutic response to spironolactone" — i.e., regional inconsistency driven by data integrity/adherence, not biology.
- **LEADER (Marso et al., NEJM 2016; Nielsen, DeChiaro & Goldman, Front Med 2021, doi:10.3389/fmed.2021.662775) — informative.** Global MACE HR 0.87 (0.78–0.97); regional HRs ranged "from a HR (95% CI) of 0.62 (0.37; 1.04) in Asia to 1.01 (0.84; 1.22) in North America," interaction p=0.20 (regional N: Europe 3,296; North America 2,847; Asia 711; ROW 2,486). FDA CDER statisticians used a **Bayesian hierarchical model** to estimate regional effects; the advisory committee "voted 17–2 to support the notion that LEADER provides substantial evidence that liraglutide reduces cardiovascular risk." Chinese authorities specifically requested a local subgroup analysis (14 MACE in 92 Chinese participants).
- **China contrasts:** nivolumab CheckMate-078 (China/Asia-anchored MRCT, approved 2018 — China's first PD-1 approval) shows the "design the pivotal trial around the region" strategy; toripalimab/sintilimab FDA rejections (ODAC Feb 10, 2022, 14:1) show the mirror-image problem when a single-country dataset is not generalizable. Denosumab (Prolia) osteoporosis used registrational real-world evidence to support China registration (PMC8688380).

**(c) Methodological gaps / open problems.** (1) *Post-selection inference for consistency:* every classical consistency method (MHLW Method 1/2 and descendants) assumes a *pre-specified* region or subgroup; none controls type-I error when the "consistent" subgroup is *discovered* post hoc. (2) *Multiplicity of the consistency claim itself*, stacked on top of subgroup multiplicity. (3) *Sample-size implications:* consistency assurance already inflates regional N; adding a data-driven subgroup shrinks it further. (4) *Estimand ambiguity:* marginal region-specific ATE vs CATE under covariate shift (the Qing et al. contribution). (5) *No regulatory type-I-error framework* for "subgroup rescue."

**(d) Practical implications for an exploratory-subgroup-search methodologist** are given in Recommendations below.

## Details

### Thread 1 — Regulatory framework and precedents

**ICH E17 (2017), "General Principles for Planning and Design of Multi-Regional Clinical Trials"** (database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E17EWG_Step4_2017_1116.pdf; FDA reprint fda.gov/media/99974/download). Core relevant principles: MRCTs are planned under the assumption the treatment effect applies to the entire target population; **structured exploration** of consistency across regions and subpopulations should be *pre-planned*; consistency is defined as "a lack of clinically relevant differences" (descriptive, not a hypothesis test). E17 introduces **pooled regions** and **pooled subpopulations** (pooling subjects across regions who share effect-modifying intrinsic/extrinsic factors per ICH E5) — precisely the conceptual license for the covariate-based subgroup argument. Secondary literature: Tanaka, Binkowitz & Wang (2018), "Multi-Regional Clinical Trials, ICH-E17, and Subpopulations," Springer ICSA (doi:10.1007/978-981-10-7829-3_11); "Basic Considerations for the Consistency Evaluation Based on ICH E17 Guideline," Ther Innov Regul Sci (doi:10.1007/s43441-024-00737-z), which notes FDA CDER's Bayesian hierarchical use in LEADER; and the FDA Sept 16, 2024 draft guidance on oncology MRCTs flagging the "decreasing proportion of US participants."

**China NMPA/CDE.** The governing document is **NMPA 2018 No. 52, 《接受药品境外临床试验数据的技术指导原则》 (Technical Guidance Principles for Accepting Overseas Clinical Trial Data, July 10, 2018).** It requires, for efficacy, that sponsors "既要从整体上确证研究药物的有效性，还要分析中国亚组与总体人群的一致性" (confirm overall efficacy AND analyze Chinese-subgroup-vs-overall consistency), invoking ICH E5 ethnic-factor analysis, with a three-tier framework (fully/partially/not accept) plus a conditional-acceptance route. Crucially, **the 2018 guidance sets no numeric consistency threshold.** The operational metric — **the Chinese-subgroup point estimate divided by the overall point estimate** ("consistency ratio," δ_China/δ_overall) — is stated verbatim in industry SAPs written to CDE requirements (e.g., pamrevlumab DMD SAP, NCT04632940, Appendix 6: "The point estimate of treatment effect in China subgroup divided by its counterpart in the overall population will be used for assessing efficacy consistency"). Related Chinese primary guidances: 《药物临床试验亚组分析指导原则（试行）》 (CDE 2020 No. 64, subgroup analysis); 《药物临床试验的生物统计学指导原则》 (2016, ICH E9-aligned); 《药物临床研究有效性综合分析指导原则（试行）》 (CDE 2021 No. 59); and the precursor 《国际多中心药物临床试验指南（试行）》 (CFDA 2015, origin of the "趋势一致性/trend consistency" language). The ≥50%-preservation figure widely applied to China is **imported by analogy from the Japanese MHLW/PMDA Method 1**, not codified in NMPA law. Pooling-strategy discussion for China: "Basic Considerations for Data Pooling Strategy in MRCTs," Ther Innov Regul Sci (PMC11880086), which recommends East-Asian pooling rather than fixating on a Chinese sample-size fraction; and the 2026 East-Asia review (doi:10.1007/s43441-026-00981-5).

**Japan MHLW/PMDA.** **MHLW, "Basic Principles on Global Clinical Trials," PFSB/ELD Notification No. 0928010 (Sept 28, 2007)** (pmda.go.jp/files/000153265.pdf). **Method 1:** choose Japanese N so that (verbatim) "D_Japan/D_all > π will occur with a probability of 80% or higher. A π should be set as an appropriate value, and 0.5 or more is generally recommended" (i.e., the Japanese effect preserves ≥50% of the global effect). **Method 2:** design so the treatment effect has the same direction in all regions. Empirical implementation: Asano, Aoi, Kamada, Uyama & Tohkin (2021), "Points to Consider for Implementation of the ICH E17 Guideline," Clin Pharmacol Ther 109(6):1555–1563 (doi:10.1002/cpt.2121), analyzing 167 MRCTs. **Taiwan TFDA** and **Korea MFDS** follow the ICH E5/E17 + MHLW-type framework; Taiwanese biostatisticians (Hsiao/Liu/Tsou group) authored much of the consistency literature; ticagrelor's Taiwan approval carried a mandatory post-approval local study; Korean submissions routinely add pre-specified Korean subgroup analyses (e.g., SUSTAIN-CHINA semaglutide protocol NCT03061214).

### Thread 4 — Data-driven subgroup identification and regulatory credibility
Method families:
- **SIDES / SIDEScreen:** Lipkovich, Dmitrienko, Denne & Enas (2011), "Subgroup identification based on differential effect search," Stat Med 30(21):2601–2621; Lipkovich & Dmitrienko (2014), SIDEScreen. Tutorial: Lipkovich, Dmitrienko & D'Agostino Sr (2017), "Data-driven subgroup identification and analysis in clinical trials," Stat Med 36(1):136–196.
- **Virtual Twins:** Foster, Taylor & Ruberg (2011), Stat Med 30:2867–2880 (includes bootstrap bias-corrected Q(Â)); modeling guidance in Deng et al. (2023), Clin Trials.
- **Model-based recursive partitioning:** Seibold, Zeileis & Hothorn (2016), Int J Biostat 12(1):45–63; **GUIDE:** Loh, He & Man (2015), Stat Med 34:1818–1833; **causal forests/GRF:** Wager & Athey (2018); Athey, Tibshirani & Wager (2019); iterative causal forest (Wang et al. 2024, Am J Epidemiol 193(5):764–776; hdiCF, Am J Epidemiol 194(7):2085–2097).
- **Forest search (survival):** **León, Dejardin & Jacqmin-Gadda (2024)**, "Exploratory subgroup identification in the heterogeneous Cox model: a relatively simple procedure," Stat Med (doi:10.1002/sim.10163) — screens all subgroups by hazard-ratio thresholds, uses splitting-consistency and a **bootstrap bias-corrected Cox estimator with jackknife variance**, benchmarked against virtual twins and GRF. Highly relevant to a survival-analysis subgroup methodologist.
- **Controlled selection:** "Data-driven controlled subgroup selection in clinical trials," arXiv:2512.15676 (2025); isotonic subgroup selection (Müller, Reeve, Cannings & Samworth).
- **Regulatory framing:** **EMA, "Guideline on the investigation of subgroups in confirmatory clinical trials"** (EMA/CHMP/539146/2013; effective Aug 1, 2019) — subgroups establish that the effect applies to the heterogeneous population; warns on false positives/negatives; signal-generation not confirmation. EFSPI/PSI white paper: Dane, Spencer, Rosenkranz, Lipkovich & Parke (2019), Pharm Stat (doi:10.1002/pst.1919), with Hemmings commentary (doi:10.1002/pst.1935). FDA: "Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness" (2019). Alosh, Huque, Bretz et al. (2017), "Tutorial on statistical considerations on subgroup analysis in confirmatory clinical trials," Stat Med 36(8):1334–1360.
- **Prospective alternative:** adaptive enrichment designs (biomarker-defined subgroup assessed at interim with enrollment restriction) — a way to convert a post-hoc subgroup argument into a pre-specified one, potentially usable for regional/consistency objectives.

### Thread 5 — Information-borrowing alternatives for region-specific inference
- **Bayesian dynamic borrowing for China bridging:** **Edwards, Best, Crawford, Zi, Shelton & Fowler (2024)**, "Using Bayesian Dynamic Borrowing to Maximize the Use of Existing Data: A Case-Study," Ther Innov Regul Sci 58:1–10 (doi:10.1007/s43441-023-00585-3) — a real CDE bridging design (robust power prior / full borrowing) where the global study enrolled zero Chinese patients. **GSK mepolizumab China bridging study (NCT03562195)** used Schmidli (2014) robust MAP dynamic borrowing from global MEA115588; Chen et al. (2024), ERJ Open Res 10(3), reported the Chinese trial (65% CSE reduction; rate ratio 0.35, 95% CI 0.24–0.50). **Zhang, Zhang & Roychoudhury (2026, Pfizer)**, "On regional treatment effect assessment using robust MAP priors," arXiv:2601.01811 — closed-form robust-MAP posterior approximation for regional consistency. Tuning-parameter guidance: "Information borrowing in Bayesian clinical trials: choice of tuning parameters for the robust mixture prior," arXiv:2412.03185 (2024).
- **Selective/covariate-aware borrowing:** "Selective Information Borrowing for Region-Specific Treatment Effect Inference under Covariate Mismatch in MRCTs," arXiv:2602.02703 (2026) — estimand-based RSATE with IVW augmentation robust to covariate shift/mismatch/outcome drift.
- **Bayesian model averaging MRCT designs:** **Bean, Ibrahim & Psioda (2021/2023)**, "Bayesian multiregional clinical trials using model averaging," Biostatistics 24(2):262–276 (doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxab027); and **Bean, Ibrahim & Psioda (2023)**, "Bayesian design of multi-regional clinical trials with time-to-event endpoints," Biometrics 79(4):3586 (doi:10.1111/biom.13820) — partitions regions into shared-effect sets, averages via posterior model probabilities, worked on LEADER; code at github.com/nwbean/MRCTs-BMA.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Position the tool honestly against the estimand and the regulator.** Build your exploratory subgroup-search tool to output, for any candidate subgroup S, both (i) the region-specific effect within S and (ii) the **consistency ratio δ_{China∩S}/δ_{global}** (MHLW Method-1 style) *and* the **CATE-standardized regional effect** (Qing et al. 2602.07468 two-step / Hua et al. calibration weighting). Frame the deliverable as *signal generation* consistent with EMA 2019 and ICH E17's descriptive-consistency stance — not a confirmatory claim. Benchmark that changes the plan: if a regulator (CDE/PMDA) will only accept pre-specified subgroups, pivot the tool's primary use to *design-stage* effect-modifier pre-specification and adaptive enrichment rather than post-hoc rescue.

**Stage 2 — Make the inference bias-corrected and reproducible.** Adopt the León/Dejardin/Jacqmin-Gadda (2024) forest-search template for survival: all-subgroup screening + splitting-consistency + **bootstrap bias-corrected Cox with jackknife variance**. Add post-selection/multiplicity control (SIDEScreen-style complexity penalties; resampling-based null distributions for the max-selected consistency statistic). Report a "consistency probability" under both fixed- and random-effects models (Ren/Xu 2508.09443; Quan 2013 shrinkage) to show the subgroup's consistency is not a shrinkage artifact. Threshold that changes the recommendation: if simulated type-I error for the consistency claim exceeds ~2.5% one-sided after correction, the subgroup route is not defensible and you should fall back to Stage 3.

**Stage 3 — Pair every subgroup argument with an information-borrowing fallback.** Because post-hoc subgroup rescue is fragile, pre-specify a Bayesian dynamic-borrowing analysis (robust MAP / power prior; Edwards et al. 2024; Zhang/Zhang/Roychoudhury 2601.01811) or BMA (Bean et al.) for the region-specific effect, tuned so borrowing down-weights automatically under prior-data conflict. This is the demonstrated CDE-accepted path (mepolizumab).

**Stage 4 — Validate against the precedent set.** Calibrate your tool on the public precedents: it should "recover" the BELIEVE BTB≤15 subgroup and should *fail to endorse* the PLATO North-America and TOPCAT Russia/Georgia subgroups (where inconsistency was aspirin-dose confounding and undetectable-drug/adherence artifact — 30% undetectable canrenone in Russia vs 3% in US/Canada — respectively). This specificity check distinguishes genuine effect modification from chance/artifact.

## Caveats
- **Preprint status:** the most on-point works (arXiv 2602.07468, 2605.16885, 2601.01811, 2602.02703, 2508.09443, 2404.08128, 2608.15450, 2512.15676) are 2024–2026 preprints, several not yet peer-reviewed; verify final published versions and check for errata (several carry disclosed industry funding — MSD/Merck, Pfizer, GSK).
- **No codified numeric China threshold:** the δ_China/δ_overall "consistency ratio" and the ≥50%-preservation figure are conventions (MHLW Method 1, industry SAPs), not binding NMPA numeric law; CDE evaluates consistency holistically and case-by-case.
- **Post-hoc vs pre-specified is the crux:** BELIEVE succeeded partly because BTB was clinically plausible as an effect modifier and produced a coherent restricted label; PLATO's post-hoc aspirin story delayed approval. A discovered subgroup carries real regulatory and type-I-error risk that current classical consistency methods do not formally price in.
- **Data-integrity confounds biology:** TOPCAT shows apparent regional inconsistency can be an adherence/fraud signal; a subgroup tool should not "rescue" consistency that is actually an artifact.
- **The FDA is now on the same side of this problem** (Sept 2024 oncology MRCT draft guidance on declining US representation), so methods framed only around China/Japan should generalize to any under-represented region.
- **Source-quality note:** PLATO has a documented reanalysis controversy (Serebruany et al.) alleging outcome misreporting; treat the aspirin-dose explanation as the sponsor/investigator account, not settled fact — which reinforces the point that post-hoc regional explanations are epistemically fragile.
