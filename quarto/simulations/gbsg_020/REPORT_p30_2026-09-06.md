## The regime, first

The design planted a 30.65% harm region, and the trials realize it (mean 0.306, SD at its binomial value). **The identifier does not return it:** the identified Ĥ averages **80 of 500** (16%) in every n = 500 cell and **132 of 1000** (13%) at n = 1000, against true harm subgroups of 153 and 306 — roughly half the planted region, every time. The `er ≤ 59` boundary is not on the search's `er` grid (44 under-covers, 69.8 over-covers), the forced cuts are `er ≤ 0`, `meno == 0`, `pgr ≤ 0`, and the consistency screen with `maxk = 2` favours the tighter, higher-effect corner of the planted region, so the realized rules are sub-regions whose conditional effect β(Ĥ) sits **below** the planted θ†(Ĥ) (mean β(Ĥ) 0.83 / 1.24 / 1.49 against θ† 1.00 / 1.50 / 1.75; 0.85 vs 1.00 at n = 1000): the sub-region the search selects on the trial's noise is not the planted region's higher-effect part on the super-population. The consequence for this task's question is direct: **the complement is still 84–87% of the trial, as at M1 (88%)**, near-tied candidates' complements still nearly coincide, and the complement's selection variability is again negligible — SD(β̃ᶜ)/naive SE = **0.98 / 1.03 / 1.06 / 1.01** and λ-SDᶜ/naive SE = **0.98 / 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.99** across the four cells. The regime the task set out to leave was not left, because the search's realized subgroup, not the planted one, sets the complement's size. That is the finding; the constructions' behaviour below is then unsurprising and consistent with 12.5%.

## Reading

Read by location and coverage, never as significance at 1.0. **Complement.** In every cell the true conditional benefit β(Ĥᶜ) is 0.77–0.88 (weaker than M1's 0.65: the identified Ĥ leaves half the planted harm region inside the complement, and the design's θ†(Ĥᶜ) is 0.72 rather than 0.66). The naive upper bound (0.84–1.00) covers only 80–83% of the time; the IJ two-term bound (1.01–1.29) covers 99.7% and, at 1.25–1.29 in the harm cells, rules out nothing; the field's upper bound sits at **0.94 (null, n = 500), 0.89 (null, n = 1000), 1.04 and 1.07 (HR 1.5, 1.75)** with 92–94% coverage — below 0.85 on 24–31% of null replicates and on 6–8% of harm-cell replicates, against a benefit truth of 0.77–0.88. The spread between the adjusted upper bounds (0.18–0.21 log-HR between field and IJ) is, as before, what the constructions pay for selection: IJ's in width, the field's in a 1–3-point coverage shortfall at n = 500. **Harm.** With naive estimates carrying +2.5 to +4.1 SD of optimism, the adjusted lower bounds sit at 0.50–0.52 (null, n = 500), 0.59 (null, n = 1000), 0.71–0.72 (HR 1.5) and 0.84–0.85 (HR 1.75), field and IJ within 0.02 of each other: no clinically meaningful harm is established at the null, and at HR 1.75 the lower bound reaches 0.85 on 40% and 0.95 on 27% of replicates — the field's one-sided coverage 0.944–0.972 and its two-sided 0.950–0.986 say those bounds are honest. **The pair.** Stating both bounds from the two separate constructions holds jointly 88–90% of the time; the Bonferroni pair holds 94–95% (0.939 at n = 1000) at 0.11–0.14 (Ĥ) and 0.03–0.04 (Ĥᶜ) log-HR of extra margin, and calibration recovers nothing because the draws are nearly independent. **What this says about promoting an unadjusted complement SE:** nothing new — the naive SE was adequate for β̃ᶜ here for the same reason it was at 12.5% (the realized complement is ~85% of the trial and its identity barely moves across re-selection), and this campaign did not produce the regime in which that would fail. To produce it, the *realized* Ĥ has to be large — which means either a planted region whose boundary the search can represent (the true `er` cut on the grid, or forced), or a design in which competing candidates have genuinely different complements; both are changes to the identifier's setting or the family, not to the DGM's prevalence alone.
