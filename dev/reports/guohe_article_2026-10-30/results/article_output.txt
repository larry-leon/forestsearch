Post-selection bounds on a nested continuum: the Guo and He (2021) design

Section S4 of León and Anderson (2026b), with the code that produces it

Author

Larry Leon

Published

October 30, 2026

User settings. These are the only values to change to run this article at another size (see Running the manuscript’s design at the end).

Guo and He (2021) treat subgroup analysis in two steps: identify the subgroup with the largest estimated effect by the argmax over a prespecified candidate family, then bound the effect of the subgroup so identified. Their post hoc design (their Section 5.2 and Table 7) runs both steps on a nested continuum of cutpoints, where neighbouring candidates differ by a single subject. It therefore shows what the argmax identifies on such a family, and what inference must then correct. Section S4 of León and Anderson (2026b) runs that design exactly as published and sets forest search’s post-selection bounds beside Guo and He’s. This article is that section with its code.

What the section compares, and why the targets coincide.

-   Guo and He’s selection is the argmax of the candidate effects. Forest search without its consistency screen reduces to the same primitive, so on this design the two methods identify the same subgroup \(\hat H\) and differ only in how they correct its effect (León and Anderson 2026a, Supplementary Section S3.5).
-   Their targets — the best subgroup effect and the best selected subgroup effect — are forest search’s two estimands, \(\log\theta^{\dagger}(H)\) for the true harm region and \(\beta(\hat H)\) for the region actually selected. Everything below is scored against \(\beta(\hat H)\), the effect of the subgroup actually selected.
-   On the selected subgroup, four one-sided 95% lower bounds on \(\beta(\hat H)\) are compared: the unadjusted bound (the within-subgroup Cox analysis of \(\hat H\) as if it had been prespecified), Guo and He’s bound at \(r = 1/12\), MR (field) and MR (IJ two-term), the conservative reference.
-   On the complement \(\hat H^{c}\), Guo and He’s framework defines no bound: the complement is not a member of their family and not the argmax of any functional. The field-s upper bound and the Bonferroni pair are shown there as a capability the comparison does not score against them.

What this article does. It carries the complete code — the data-generating mechanism, the nested family, the truth curve, the four bounds, the complement and the pair, the Monte Carlo loop and the summaries — in a form a reader can run. Its own run is small (2 cells, 25 replicates each, Guo and He at \(B\) = 200), enough to exercise every path and draw the figure. The manuscript’s results, from 2,000 replicates per cell at \(B\) = 2000, are then given in full so the reader has them in mind. The seeds follow the manuscript’s own scheme, so a run at its settings reproduces its per-replicate records, not only its summaries.

Bounds are read by their location on the hazard-ratio scale against one and against the planted effect: a lower bound on the hazard ratio in \(\hat H\) above one is on the harm side; an upper bound on the hazard ratio in \(\hat H^{c}\) below one is on the benefit side. The locations reported are where the bounds sit, not claims.

The design

Guo and He’s Section 5.2 generates \(n = 400\) subjects with \(W \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0, 80]\) and treatment \(D \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)\) from the proportional hazards model \(\lambda_0(t)\exp\{b(W)D\}\) with a unit exponential baseline hazard, \(b(w) = \beta_2\) for \(w \le 30\) and \(0\) otherwise, and censoring with \(\log C \sim \mathrm{Unif}(-1.25, 1.00)\), about 40% across the cells; \(\beta_2 \in \{0, 0.1, \ldots, 0.5\}\). The true harm region is \(H = \{W \le 30\}\), about 150 subjects. The candidates are \(S(c) = \{W \le c\}\) for \(c \in [30, 60]\). Because an estimate changes only where \(c\) passes an observed value of \(W\), the family is taken to be the distinct cuts at 30 and at every observed \(w \in (30, 60]\) — nested, cut at every observed value, about 151 candidates per replicate. This discretisation is the manuscript’s; Guo and He do not state one.

One trial, to see the objects:

The truth: \(\beta(c)\) along the continuum

\(\beta(c)\) is the Cox log hazard ratio of treatment within \(S(c)\): a weighted average of \(b(\cdot)\) over \([0, c]\), the working model’s least-false value under the censoring the design imposes. Two facts anchor it: \(\beta(30) = \beta_2\) exactly, because \(b\) is constant on \([0, 30]\); and \(\beta(c)\) is non-increasing for \(c > 30\), diluted by the null region alone. At \(\beta_2 = 0\) the curve is exactly zero everywhere. For \(\beta_2 > 0\) there is no closed form, and the curve is computed from one large sample by a prefix sweep over a grid of \(c\), with the isotonic non-increasing projection as the interpolation basis. The manuscript uses \(n_{\mathrm{big}} = 2 \times 10^6\) on a 0.25 grid; this article uses 200,000 on a unit grid, which costs a few seconds and places \(\beta(c)\) within about 0.006 of the manuscript’s curve.

The four bounds on \(\hat H\), and the complement

Everything the comparison needs is read from two calls on one trial.

-   guohe_algorithm3() is the package’s independent implementation of Guo and He’s Algorithm 3: deterministic offsets \(d(c) = (1 - n^{\,r - 1/2})\{\hat\gamma_{\max} - \hat\beta(c)\}\) are added to every candidate, the maximum is re-taken over bootstrap samples, and a bootstrap quantile of the re-centred maximum is subtracted from \(\hat\gamma_{\max}\); the bias-reduced estimate subtracts the bootstrap mean instead. orient = +1 makes a larger coefficient the effect of interest; \(r = 1/12\) is the manuscript’s reported value (its other values are sensitivity only); \(B\) is the manuscript’s own choice, since Guo and He state none for their Section 5.
-   fs_mr_inference() is multiplier resampling over the fixed family: each draw perturbs every candidate estimate through one shared multiplier vector and re-applies the argmax (reselection = "maxeff", with no admission floor). From the same draws it returns the two-term de-biased estimate \(\tilde\beta(\hat H)\) with its infinitesimal-jackknife bound, MR (IJ two-term); the field, which inverts around \(\tilde\beta(\hat H)\) the law of the selection discrepancy \(\Lambda^{*}\) — its bound is \(\tilde\beta(\hat H) - q_{0.95}(\Lambda^{*})\) and its point estimate subtracts the mean of \(\Lambda^{*}\) (R_out outer and R_in inner draws); the complement’s two-term estimate and its field-s upper bound, studentized to the selected complement’s own scale; and the Bonferroni pair, the field lower bound on \(\hat H\) and the field-s upper bound on \(\hat H^{c}\) each at one-sided 0.975, for a joint claim at level at least 0.95. The re-selection frequency \(\hat p(\hat H)\) — the share of draws whose argmax is \(\hat H\) itself — comes with it.
-   The unadjusted bounds are the standard analyses as if the regions had been prespecified: the model-based Cox standard error on \(\hat H\), the robust standard error on \(\hat H^{c}\), as in the manuscript.

The seeds are the manuscript’s: a cell’s base seed is a function of its label, the data of replicate \(m\) use base \(+ m\), Guo and He’s bootstrap base \(+ m + 500{,}000\) and the multiplier draws base \(+ m + 700{,}000\), so a run at the manuscript’s settings reproduces its records replicate for replicate.

The argmax selected \(\hat c\) = 35.5, an overshoot of 5.5 beyond the true boundary, so \(\hat H\) holds 176 subjects against about 150 in \(H\) and its true hazard ratio is 1.28, diluted from the planted 1.35. The three identifications agree on this replicate: Guo and He’s argmax and the multiplier resampling both carry the same candidate. The four lower bounds sit where the section says they typically sit: the unadjusted highest, the IJ two-term lowest, Guo and He’s and the field between them and close to each other. On the complement, whose true hazard ratio is exactly one on this design, the field-s upper bound is 1.31 and the unadjusted 1.22; the pair places the hazard ratio in \(\hat H\) at or above 1.01 and in \(\hat H^{c}\) at or below 1.35, which overlap — the two groups are not separated by this trial.

The Monte Carlo

The loop runs bounds_one() over replicates, in parallel across 2 workers (the seeds are fixed per replicate, so the results do not depend on the plan). Coverage is scored against \(\beta(\hat H)\) for the selected subgroup and against exactly zero for the complement — every \(\hat H^{c} = \{W > \hat c\}\) lies inside \(\{W > 30\}\), where \(b = 0\) — so a complement bound covers when it is at least zero.

Read against one, every typical lower bound lies below the selected subgroup’s hazard ratio in its cell. Guo and He’s published Table 7 is more conservative still than the manuscript’s replication of it: at \(r = 1/12\) the published coverages run 0.961 to 0.972, about 0.01 above the replicated columns, a deficit the manuscript carries unchanged.

Limits

The manuscript records these; they bound what the design can show.

1.  The field’s low points are under-correction on a nested family. Its harm-side coverage bottoms out at 0.933 (\(\beta_2 = 0.1\)), with its point estimate biased upward in every cell, and the field-s complement bound bottoms out at 0.928 in the null cell. Both sit where the pick is least settled: re-selection is spread thin throughout, with median \(\hat p(\hat H)\) from 0.196 to 0.309. Across Guo and He’s replicated designs the nested family is the hardest case for the field’s second-order correction.
2.  Theory scope. The design lies inside Guo and He’s theory. Its roughly 151 candidates, each differing from its neighbour by one subject, are the near-duplicate case that strains the no-tie condition on which the multiplier-resampling theory rests, so the field’s calibration here is an empirical finding on this design, not a consequence of that theory.
3.  Identification is flattered by construction. The true boundary is the family’s smallest member, toward which the argmax is drawn even when there is no harm region; for \(\beta_2 > 0\) sensitivity and negative predictive value are identically one, and overshoot is the only error.
4.  Forest search’s screen and family construction are not exercised. The identifier is the pure argmax over Guo and He’s family. Forest search’s screened identifier is evaluated in Section 5 of the manuscript and in vignette("forestsearch").
5.  A benefit claim is untested. The complement’s truth is zero in every cell, so no cell holds a benefit for a bound to establish.
6.  One design, one sample size. Everything is at \(n = 400\) on Guo and He’s Section 5.2 design.
7.  Guo and He are not run on a screened family. Their method is applied exactly as published, on the family their Section 5.2 specifies; their suggestion that it serves as a conservative approach after other identification is not tested, and no claim is made either way.
8.  No adaptive choice of \(r\). Guo and He’s Table 7 reports none, and neither does the manuscript: in a pilot of their cross-validated choice on this design, changing only the seed of the adaptive procedure changed \(\hat r\) on 5 of 10 replicates with the data and the selection unchanged.
9.  Everything is one-sided. Every construction compared is a one-sided bound, so nothing depends on the choice of a two-sided interval method.

Everything else follows: the seeds reproduce the manuscript’s replicates, the summaries and figures are those of Section S4 at full size, and the reference tables above are what the run should reproduce up to Monte Carlo error of the truth curve (a few thousandths on the log scale at \(n_{\mathrm{big}} = 2 \times 10^6\)).

The cost is Guo and He’s bootstrap. On one core of a Linux workstation, one replicate at these settings takes about 72 s, of which the bootstrap at \(B = 2000\) is about 70 s and the multiplier resampling with its field about 2 s; the six cells at 2,000 replicates are therefore about 240 core-hours — some 2.5 hours on 100 workers, or a weekend on 8. At \(B = 200\) a replicate takes about 10 s. The truth curve at \(n_{\mathrm{big}} = 2 \times 10^6\) costs about a minute per cell, once.
